## OKR Framework

### How We Set OKRs

1. **Founder sets 3–5 company objectives** (annual, reviewed quarterly)
2. **Each facet lead sets 2–3 team objectives** aligned to company objectives
3. **Each team member sets 2–3 personal objectives** aligned to team objectives
4. **Key results are measurable** (numbers, not feelings)
5. **Review monthly, score quarterly** (0.0–1.0 scale, target 0.7)

### Scoring

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1.0 | Delivered beyond expectation (sandbagged? aim higher next time) |
| 0.7 | Delivered on target (sweet spot) |
| 0.5 | Partially delivered |
| 0.3 | Made some progress |
| 0.0 | Didn't start / failed |

## Strategic Decision Framework

### When to Say Yes
- Does it align with our vision (trade businesses, AI receptionist)?
- Will it meaningfully impact a key metric (MRR, NPS, churn)?
- Can we execute it with current team + resources?
- Is the timing right?

### When to Say No
- It doesn't serve our core customer (Australian trade businesses)
- It distracts from the 12-month roadmap
- It requires capabilities we don't have (and can't build quickly)
- It's a "nice to have" not a "must have"

### When to Pivot
- Churn is consistently >5% despite intervention
- Customers consistently say they'd be "very disappointed" without us <40%
- CAC > LTV after 6 months of optimisation
- Market isn't responding (no organic growth after 100 outreach)
